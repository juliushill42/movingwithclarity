God, Give Me a Sign

GOD, GIVE ME A SIGN.

There comes a point where even airing your grief feels exhausting. Like you're a broken record, tired of hearing your own voice repeat the same prayer you've been praying for God knows how long. And still… you pray it again.

I've said that more times than I can count. Not because I've stopped believing. Because sometimes believing is the only thing I have left.

You only complain to somebody you still secretly hope is listening. You don't keep talking to somebody you believe is completely gone. You keep talking because somewhere deep down, you still believe they can hear you.

So God, give me a sign. Not because I'm ready to quit. Because I want to know if I'm walking in the right direction.

Because sometimes the sign isn't the mountain moving. Sometimes it's realizing you're stronger than you were when you first climbed it. Sometimes the answer isn't a door opening. Sometimes it's the strength to keep knocking.

Why a domain, not a shelf

The book is the infrastructure.

A1

You own the shelf

No algorithm, no store policy, no account suspension between the reader and the words. The domain is yours the way the manuscript is yours.

D4

It stays alive

A Kindle listing is a file. A living book can grow — new chapters, annotations, a reader's own notes — without a new edition or a new ISBN.

H8

It compounds

Every book on its own domain is another piece of territory. Point them at each other and the shelf becomes a network — the moat gets wider with each release.

If this made you want to put your book on your domain —

that's the point. This page is the first build of a template for turning any manuscript into a living, ownable site instead of a rented listing. Book I is already fully typeset — this is what comes after the file.
